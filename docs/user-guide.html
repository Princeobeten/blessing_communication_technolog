1. What this software does

This is a Computer Repair Management System (CRMS). It replaces the paper records and scattered spreadsheets a repair workshop normally uses. Everything lives in one place:

  • Customers — who brought a device in, and their contact details.
  • Repair jobs — what came in, what is wrong with it, who is fixing it and how far along it is.
  • Spare parts — what is in stock, what has been used, and what needs reordering.
  • Invoices — what the customer owes and what they have paid.
  • Reports — how the workshop is performing.

The system also messages the customer automatically every time their repair moves forward, and gives them a page where they can check the status themselves.

4. The repair process, start to finish

Every repair follows the same path through the system:

#StageWhat happens
1Device comes inYou fill the intake form. The system creates a tracking number and texts/emails the customer.
2Technician diagnosesThe technician inspects it and writes down what is actually wrong.
3Quote is sentYou enter labour and parts cost. The customer is told the price.
4Customer approvesYou record their approval. Only then can work start.
5Repair is doneParts are fitted — stock reduces automatically. Work is written up.
6Invoice and paymentThe invoice builds itself from the labour and parts recorded. You take payment.
7Device handed backMark it delivered. The job is closed.
The system protects you. You cannot skip a stage, cannot start work before the customer approves the price, cannot invoice a repair that is not finished, cannot use a part you do not have, and cannot take more money than the invoice is for. If you try, it explains why in plain words.

5. Everyday tasks, step by step

A. Logging a device that has just come in

  1. Click Repair jobs, then the blue New repair intake button.
  2. Under Customer, choose New customer and type their name, phone and email — or choose Existing customer and pick them from the list.
  3. Under Device details, record the type, brand, model, serial number, anything they left with it (charger, bag) and any existing damage.
  4. Under Reported fault, write the problem in the customer's own words.
  5. On the right, set the priority, assign a technician if you know who will handle it, and set an expected completion date.
  6. Click Create work order.
Give the customer their tracking number. It looks like TRK-2026-0014 and appears at the top of the job. They use it to check progress themselves without phoning you.

The Repair jobs list. Search by ticket, customer, brand or serial number.

B. Working on a repair

Click any ticket number to open its work screen. Everything about that repair is here.

  1. Move it forward. The Repair status box at the top shows only the stages you are allowed to move to next. Click one, add a note for the customer, and confirm.
  2. Write your findings. Fill in Technician diagnosis and Work carried out, set the labour charge, then click Save.
  3. Fit a part. In Spare parts used, click Add part, pick it and enter how many. Stock drops automatically. The bin icon returns a part to stock if you added it by mistake.
  4. Check the history. Job history at the bottom shows every change, who made it and when.

The repair work screen. Customer, device and running cost stay on the right.

C. Quoting a price and getting approval

  1. On the repair screen, click Prepare quote (top right).
  2. Enter the labour cost and the estimated parts cost. The total is worked out for you.
  3. Click Send quote. The job moves to Awaiting approval and the customer is told the price.
  4. When the customer agrees, click Approve quote, type who approved it, and confirm.
  5. The job moves to In progress on its own. Work can now begin.

D. Billing and taking payment

  1. Finish the repair and mark it Completed.
  2. Click Generate invoice. The invoice is built automatically from the labour charge and the parts you recorded — you do not retype anything.
  3. Add a tax rate or discount if needed, then click Generate.
  4. Open the invoice and click Record payment. Enter the amount and how they paid. Part payments are fine — the balance updates.
  5. Click Print to give the customer a copy.
  6. When they collect the device, go back to the repair and mark it Delivered.

An invoice, ready to print.

E. Looking after spare parts

  1. Click Inventory to see every part and how many are left.
  2. Add part registers something new. The reorder level is the number at which the system starts warning you.
  3. Adjust changes the stock count. Enter a positive number for stock received (e.g. 10) or a negative number to write stock off (e.g. -2), and give a reason.
  4. Tick Low stock only to see just the parts that need reordering.
Parts fitted to a repair come off stock automatically — you never adjust for those by hand. Every single change is recorded with a reason, so the count can always be explained.

Inventory. Red and amber tags mark parts that are out or running low.

F. Registering a customer on their own

You normally do not need this — the intake form registers a new customer for you. But if you want to add someone in advance, click Customers then Register customer. Opening a customer shows every repair they have ever brought in.

H. Managing staff Administrator only

  1. Click Staff, then Add staff.
  2. Enter their name, email, a password of at least 6 characters, and choose Technician or Administrator.
  3. Use Disable when someone leaves — this keeps their work history but stops them signing in.

A technician with open jobs cannot be deleted. Reassign their jobs first. You also cannot remove your own administrator access, so you can never lock yourself out.

6. Repair statuses explained

The coloured tag on every job tells you where it is.

StatusMeaning
PendingDevice received, waiting for a technician to look at it.
DiagnosingA technician is inspecting it now.
Awaiting approvalThe price has been sent. Waiting for the customer to say yes.
In progressApproved, and the repair is being done.
Awaiting partsPaused while a spare part is sourced.
CompletedFixed and ready for the customer to collect.
DeliveredHanded back. The job is closed.
CancelledThe repair will not go ahead.

7. How customers track their own repair

Customers never sign in and never need an account. Give them their tracking number and this address:

https://blessing-communication-technolog.vercel.app/track

  1. They type in their tracking number, for example TRK-2026-0005.
  2. They see the current status, the device, who is repairing it, the quote and the balance owed.
  3. They see a progress list of every stage completed so far.

They see only their own repair — no other customer's details, no internal notes and no staff information. On top of this, the system emails them automatically at every stage, so most of the time they do not need to check at all. Everything it has sent is listed under Notifications.

What the customer sees. No login, no account, no phone call needed.

9. Problems and answers

What you seeWhat to do
"Invalid email or password."Check for typing mistakes and stray spaces. The password is case sensitive.
You keep being sent back to the sign-in screenYour 8-hour session ran out. Sign in again.
"A job cannot move from X to Y."Repairs must follow the proper order. Use one of the buttons the system offers you.
"The customer must approve the quote before work can begin."Click Approve quote and record who approved it first.
"Only 3 of that part left in stock."You asked for more than you have. Reduce the quantity or add stock under Inventory.
"Complete the repair before generating an invoice."Mark the repair Completed, then invoice it.
"That is more than the outstanding balance."You cannot take more than is owed. Check the balance shown on the invoice.
"This customer has 3 repair jobs and cannot be deleted."Deliberate — deleting them would destroy repair history.
"No repair found with that tracking number."Check the number. It looks like TRK-2026-0005.
Reports or Staff are missing from the menuYou are signed in as a technician. Those are administrator-only.
